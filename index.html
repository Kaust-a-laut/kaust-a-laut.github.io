Rigorous bounds

  1. [1] pcsite(ℤ3) ≥ 0.263376: a rigorous lower bound for site percolation on the cubic lattice.

    Computer-assisted proof. Re-checked by three independent AI review agents (one reproduced the computation with its own code). Not yet peer-reviewed.

    Previous best: pc > 0.2522 (Georgakopoulos and Panagiotis 2023) — best published value found as of 2026-10-19T19:07:43Z, in A. Georgakopoulos and C. Panagiotis, Combin. Probab. Comput. 32 (2023) 912–955, Theorem 7.1 (arXiv:2107.06272, eq. (16); DOI: 10.1017/S0963548323000214). [Proof (PDF)] [Code, certificate and checker] [package README] [Errata] [Also submitted as a draft to the V3R1T4S open-results repository (PR #571)]

    In words: the critical probability of site percolation on the simple cubic lattice is at least 263376/106 = 0.263376. The best published rigorous lower bound was 0.2522 (Georgakopoulos and Panagiotis, 2023, from lattice-animal counting). Numerically pc is about 0.3116.

    How it works (object/PROOF.md). Follow the shortest open paths from the origin along a canonical breadth-first tree: each site's parent is its neighbour one step closer to the origin, chosen in the fixed order +x, -x, +y, -y, +z, -z. A path in this tree is a neighbour-avoiding walk. Some sites next to it must be closed: every off-path site adjacent to two path sites at least 3 steps apart, and, at about half of the turns, the corner site, which would otherwise have been the preferred parent. If the origin's cluster is infinite, the tree has an infinite ray (Koenig's lemma). Its first N steps are such a path, and the rest is an infinite open path that avoids the path and its closed sites. Independence then gives theta(p) ≤ K_N(p) theta(p), where K_N(p) = sum_f c_f p^(N+1) (1-p)^f. The integers c_f come from an exhaustive enumeration of all N-step walks (N = 20: 3,516,072,837,894 walks, enumerated as sign-flip orbits). K_20(263376/106) = 0.99997153 < 1 in exact rational arithmetic, so theta = 0 there and pc ≥ 0.263376.

    Torquato and Jiao (2013) state 5/19 = 0.263158 for this threshold without proof (they prove 1/6). The certificate gives K_20(5/19) = 0.98476 < 1, so 5/19 is now a proved lower bound as well.

    Builds on: A. Georgakopoulos and C. Panagiotis (Combin. Probab. Comput. 32 (2023) 912-955, DOI: 10.1017/S0963548323000214, arXiv:1908.03426 and arXiv:2107.06272, the record compared against); G. Barequet and M. Shalah (arXiv:1906.11447, the polycube growth-constant bound a(ℤ3) < 9.3835 behind that record); S. Torquato and Y. Jiao (Phys. Rev. E 87, 032149 (2013), arXiv:1302.0332, the stated, unproven 5/19); A. Poenitz and P. Tittmann (Electron. J. Combin. 7 (2000) R21, the connective-constant bound 4.7387 behind the older 1/mu = 0.21103); The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences, A174319 (neighbour-avoiding walks on the simple cubic lattice, an external anchor for the walk counts).

    Not claimed
    • Not the value of pc, and nothing above 0.263376. Larger N would raise the bound. Ratio estimates suggest this forced structure levels off near 0.278; those are estimates, not results.
    • Nothing about bond percolation, other lattices or other dimensions.
    • No proof of Torquato and Jiao's method. The only claim is that their stated 5/19 lies below the bound proved here.
    • Monte Carlo confidence intervals (for example Ball 2013) are not bounds and are not compared.
    • The upper bound pcsite(ℤ3) ≤ 0.4427 is a separate submission.
    • The run's how-we-built-it/PREREG.md names 0.21103 as the record. The panel found the higher record 0.2522, and the claim is compared against that (panel/gate.md).

  2. [2] pcsite(ℤ3) ≤ 0.4427: an explicit upper bound for site percolation on the cubic lattice.

    Computer-assisted proof. Re-checked by three independent AI review agents (one reproduced the computation with its own code). Not yet peer-reviewed.

    Previous best: pc < 1/2 (Campanino-Russo 1985), listed as 0,5000 (*) in the table cited here — best published value found as of 2026-10-19T00:12:39Z (and 2026-10-18T21:31:19Z), in Gomes, Pereira, Sanchis, J. Appl. Probab. (arXiv:2106.10388, v2, 13 March 2026, DOI: 10.1017/jpr.2025.10041), Table 1, non-oriented site percolation, d = 3. [Proof (PDF)] [Code, certificate and checker] [package README] [Also submitted as a draft to the V3R1T4S open-results repository (PR #481)]

    In words: the critical probability of site percolation on the simple cubic lattice is at most 4427/10000. The best published rigorous upper bound was Campanino and Russo's pc < 1/2 (1985), with no explicit constant.

    The proof (object/PROOF.md) tiles ℤ3 by 3x3x3 blocks, follows a block growth process in the style of van den Berg and Ermakov (1996), and reduces "each step dominates Bernoulli(q)" to 6 x 511 laws checked against all 7579 nontrivial up-sets of {0,1}5 (Strassen's criterion) over all 227 block states, in outward-rounded interval arithmetic with an exact integer comparison. A chain of seven such certificates goes from theta(0.501) > 0 (by Campanino-Russo's pc ≤ 1/2) down to p = 0.4427.

    Builds on: M. Campanino and L. Russo (1985, Ann. Probab. 13, 478-491, the base input pc ≤ 1/2); J. van den Berg and A. Ermakov (1996, Random Structures Algorithms 8, 199-212, the growth-process and Strassen-coupling method); V. Strassen (1965, Ann. Math. Statist. 36, 423-439); P. A. Gomes, A. Pereira and R. Sanchis (arXiv:2106.10388, the table compared against); G. R. Grimmett (arXiv:1604.04849, the record's status); J. C. Wierman (1995, Combin. Probab. Comput. 4, 181-188) and N. Ball (arXiv:1312.0961).

    Not claimed
    • Not the value of pc, and not a bound below 0.4427. This block and rule have a fixed point near 0.4426.
    • Nothing about bond percolation (its cubic-lattice bound 0.347297, Yu and Wierman 2021, is a different model).
    • Confidence intervals (Ball 2013) are not compared: they are not bounds.
    • The run also certified a variant chain starting from q = 0.68, which needs only pcsite(ℤ2) ≤ 0.679492 (Wierman 1995) and ℤ2 inside ℤ3 instead of Campanino-Russo (how-we-built-it/outputs/chainW_summary_unpanelled.txt). That variant was done after the panel and is not part of the panelled claim.
    • No statement about higher dimensions, although bounds derived from the ℤ3 value (Gomes-Pereira-Sanchis, Theorem 2.3) would move.

  3. [3] pcsite(ℤ2) ≥ 0.5567: a rigorous lower bound for site percolation on the square lattice.

    Computer-assisted proof. Re-checked by three independent AI review agents (one reproduced the computation with its own code). Not yet peer-reviewed.

    Previous best: 0.556 — best published value found as of 2026-10-18T18:50:24Z to 18:54:23Z, in J. van den Berg and A. Ermakov, Random Structures Algorithms 8 (1996) 199-212 (DOI: 10.1002/(SICI)1098-2418(199605)8:3<199::AID-RSA4>3.0.CO;2-T). [Proof (PDF)] [Code, certificate and checker] [package README] [Also submitted as a draft to the V3R1T4S open-results repository (PR #458)]

    The previous best published rigorous lower bound is 0.556 (J. van den Berg and A. Ermakov, 1996). The proof (object/PROOF.md) keeps their growth-process comparison. Their conditioning window is replaced by exact belief propagation over the exploration tree: exact to depth 3, with outer cones covering the rest of the history. The finite part is an exact rational check at p = 4433/10000 (object/window.py, object/bound.json), proving pcsite(ℤ2) ≥ 5567/10000 = 0.5567.

    Builds on: J. van den Berg and A. Ermakov (1996, Random Structures Algorithms 8, 199-212, the method and the bound improved) and A. Ermakov; M. V. Menshikov and K. D. Pelikh (1989, Mat. Zametki 46, 38-47; 1990, Math. Notes 46, 778-785, the original method); M. E. Fisher (1961, J. Math. Phys. 2, 620-627, pc(S) + pc(S*) ≥ 1); V. Strassen via T. M. Liggett (1985, Interacting Particle Systems, Chap. II, Thm 2.4, the coupling theorem); context values by J. C. Wierman and Oberly (2024, DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-62166-6_9) and J. L. Jacobsen (2015, arXiv:1507.03027).

    Not claimed
    • No upper bound. The best upper bound found is 0.666894 (Wierman and Oberly 2024), untouched.
    • Nothing about the numerical value of pc (about 0.59274605, Jacobsen 2015), which is not a rigorous bound.
    • Not the best this method can give. The same relaxation passes up to p = 0.443214911 at depth 3, and saturates at 0.556788090 by depth 8. Only p = 4433/10000 was frozen in the preregistration, so only 0.5567 is claimed.
    • The proof rests on two classical theorems as cited: Fisher's inequality pc(S) + pc(S*) ≥ 1, and Strassen's coupling theorem.
    • See ERRATA.md for small corrections to the panelled text. None changes the result.

  1. [4] Site percolation threshold of 2-uniform lattice #23: pc = 0.57176454(10).

    Numerical estimate with error bar, not a theorem. Re-checked by three independent AI review agents. Not yet peer-reviewed.

    Previous best: 0.5720(2) — best published value found as of 2026-10-18T05:11:54Z (and 2026-10-17T23:58:23Z), in R. A. Neher, K. Mecke and H. Wagner, J. Stat. Mech. (2008), arXiv:0708.3250 (table in arXiv:0708.3251), as listed in row 23 of the "2-uniform lattices" table on Wikipedia's Percolation threshold page. [Code, certificate and checker] [package README] [Also submitted as a draft to the V3R1T4S open-results repository (PR #391)]

    For 2-uniform lattice #23, (1/2)(33,42)+(1/2)(44), the site percolation threshold is 0.57176454 +- 0.00000010 by the frozen transfer-matrix extrapolation (n ≤ 16), and it agrees within 1.53 combined sigma with an independent Monte Carlo wrapping-probability value of 0.5717667 +- 0.0000014 on tori up to 512 x 128 cells, while the same transfer-matrix code reproduces the table's citation-needed #23 bond value 0.45844622(40) to 2e-7 and the exact kagome and (3,122) site thresholds, the value lies 1.2 table sigma from the table's 0.5720(2), and the recorded literature search found no published #23 site value more precise than 2e-4.

    One cell: row 23 of the "2-uniform lattices" table on Wikipedia's "Percolation threshold" page, lattice (1/2)(33,42) + (1/2)(44) (rows of squares and triangles in the pattern S S S T), site column. The table lists 0.5720(2) (Neher, Mecke and Wagner 2008); this value has a one-sigma uncertainty about 1900 times smaller and lies 1.2 of the table's sigma below it.

    Builds on: R. A. Neher, K. Mecke and H. Wagner (2008, J. Stat. Mech., arXiv:0708.3250, table in arXiv:0708.3251); J. L. Jacobsen (arXiv:1507.03027, arXiv:1401.7847); C. R. Scullard and J. L. Jacobsen (arXiv:1209.1451); M. E. J. Newman and R. M. Ziff (arXiv:cond-mat/0005264); P. N. Suding and R. M. Ziff (arXiv:cond-mat/9811416).

    Not claimed
    • No rigorous bound: sigma_TM = 1.04e-7 is the spread of the frozen extrapolation family. On Jacobsen's square-site sequence the same rule's error is at most 0.02 sigma at every cut n_max = 10..21. The independent Monte Carlo value 0.5717667(14) tests it only at the 1.4e-6 level, and lies 1.53 combined sigma above it.
    • The Monte Carlo fit uses tori up to 512 x 128 cells. The bank's "tori up to L ≥ 2048" is read as an example (it follows "for example"); the one 2048 x 512 torus is a low-statistics consistency check (sigma 2.3e-5), not in the fit.
    • Criterion (c), bond half: the bond value reproduced is the table's 0.45844622(40) for row 23, which the table marks "citation needed"; no paper is named for it.
    • No values for the other two-uniform lattices are claimed here. Interim transfer-matrix values for rows 20-22 are in how-we-built-it/ only as run outputs. The row 22 bond value is the subject of a separate submission by another contributor (PR #239), not of this one.
